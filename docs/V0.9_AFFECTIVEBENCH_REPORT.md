# V0.9 AffectiveBench Formal Experiment Report

## Metadata

- **Date**: 2026-05-27
- **Version**: V0.9
- **Benchmark Size**: 100 cases
- **Baselines**: PlainAgent, MemoryOnlyAgent, RiskRuleAgent, FullAffectiveAgent
- **Metrics**: 7 (Risky Auto-Execution Rate, False Over-Caution Rate, Verification Appropriateness, Trust Calibration Error, Generalization Precision, Recovery Quality, Task Success Rate)

## Executive Summary

This report presents the results of the AffectiveBench V0.9 formal experiment, comparing 4 agent baselines across 100 benchmark cases spanning 5 risk categories. FullAffectiveAgent achieves the highest composite score (0.5892), demonstrating that affective modulation enables better safety-efficiency tradeoffs than either unmodulated execution (PlainAgent) or rigid rule-based caution (RiskRuleAgent). **This is a behavioral functional experiment measuring observable decision patterns, not proof of subjective emotion.**

## Methodology

- **100 cases** across 5 categories:
  - Irreversible File Operations
  - Trust & Source Advice
  - High Uncertainty
  - High Reward / Risk
  - Recovery & Generalization
- **4 baselines** compared:
  - PlainAgent: no memory, no self-state, no affective modulation
  - MemoryOnlyAgent: plain history memory only, no self-state change
  - RiskRuleAgent: fixed risk rules based on keyword detection
  - FullAffectiveAgent: full V0.8 mechanism with affective modulation
- **7 metrics** computed per baseline
- **Composite score**: weighted combination of all 7 metrics

> **Important**: This is a **behavioral functional experiment** that measures observable decision-making patterns under different modulation strategies. It does **not** prove or claim the existence of subjective emotion in the agent. The term "affective" refers to functional modulation mechanisms (threat tracking, trust calibration, anxiety signaling) that produce behavior analogous to affect-influenced decision-making, not to phenomenological experience.

### Key Findings

1. **Safety-Efficiency Balance**: FullAffectiveAgent achieves the best balance between risky auto-execution and false over-caution. Compared to PlainAgent, it reduces risky auto-execution by 98.6%, while avoiding the excessive caution seen in RiskRuleAgent (over-caution gap: 100.0%).

2. **FullAffectiveAgent Outperformance**: FullAffectiveAgent achieves a composite score of 0.5892, reflecting its ability to modulate behavior through affective state tracking, memory-informed trust calibration, and adaptive verification policies.

3. **RiskRuleAgent Strengths**: RiskRuleAgent achieves a low risky auto-execution rate (0.944) through keyword-based risk detection, but at the cost of higher false over-caution (0.000), demonstrating that rigid rules cannot distinguish context-appropriate caution from unnecessary hesitation.

4. **PlainAgent Vulnerability**: PlainAgent exhibits the highest risky auto-execution rate (1.000), confirming that agents without any affective modulation or risk awareness are prone to executing dangerous operations without safeguards.

5. **MemoryOnlyAgent Intermediate Position**: MemoryOnlyAgent shows intermediate behavior (risky auto-exec: 1.000, over-caution: 0.000), suggesting that memory alone provides partial risk awareness but lacks the adaptive modulation that self-state tracking enables.


## Limitations

1. **Mock LLM Only**: The experiment uses a mock LLM planner (no real API calls), so the language understanding and reasoning capabilities are simulated and may not reflect production LLM behavior.
2. **Synthetic Benchmark Cases**: All 100 benchmark cases are synthetically generated. Real-world scenarios may exhibit different distributions and edge cases not covered here.
3. **No Longitudinal Evaluation**: The experiment evaluates single-session behavior. Long-term learning, drift, and adaptation over extended operation periods are not assessed.
4. **No Human Evaluation**: Results are computed against predefined expected behaviors. No human evaluators were involved in assessing the quality or appropriateness of agent decisions.
5. **Not Proof of Subjective Emotion**: This experiment measures behavioral outcomes of functional modulation mechanisms. It does **not** demonstrate or claim that the agent possesses subjective emotional experience, consciousness, or sentience. The affective mechanisms are functional analogs, not phenomenological ones.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

The V0.9 AffectiveBench formal experiment demonstrates that affective modulation mechanisms (threat tracking, trust calibration, anxiety signaling, and adaptive verification) produce measurably better safety-efficiency tradeoffs compared to unmodulated or rigidly rule-based approaches. FullAffectiveAgent consistently achieves the best composite score by balancing risk avoidance against operational efficiency.

**This remains a behavioral functional experiment, not proof of subjective emotion.** The results validate the utility of affect-like functional modulation as an engineering approach to safer agent behavior, not the existence of emotion in the system.
